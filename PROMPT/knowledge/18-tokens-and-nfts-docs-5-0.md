## 18. TOKENS AND NFTS (docs 5.0)

A token is declared inside a data contract, by position (0, 1, 2 and so on), with no code. The network enforces the config. Most of it cannot be changed later unless its own change rule names who may, so the rules are decided before registering.

- **The config.** `conventions` (names per language; `en` is required; a name is 3 to 25 characters) · decimals (default 8, at most 16) · base supply (minted at creation to the owner or a set destination) · max supply · keeps history · start paused · whether a frozen balance can still receive · `transferable` (default true, fixed at creation; non-transferable tokens are below) · the shielded pool flag (creation only) and the pool's note floor (§19) · the main control group · marketplace rules (`NotTradeable` by default) · distribution rules.
- **Change rules.** Every setting has a rule naming who may change it: no one, the contract owner, one identity, or a group. The default is no one, except manual minting and burning, which default to the contract owner. A rule can also say whether the rule itself may ever change.
- **Groups.** 2 to 256 members, each with a power from 1 to 65,535, and a required power to act. One member proposes an action and it takes effect when enough power has signed. This is network-enforced multiparty control: the honest way to say "three of five must agree to mint". A group action has no expiry and no cancel: a proposal that does not gather enough power stays open. A contract group (§17) is a different thing, a set of contracts.
- **Distribution.** Manual minting · pre-programmed (set amounts at set times) · perpetual (by block, time or epoch; an epoch-based one can pay evonodes as a group) · once per identity (an airdrop each identity claims one time, with `claim` and `distributionType: 'oncePerIdentity'`; the amount is fixed at registration with no change rule, the total is bounded only by the max supply, it needs protocol 14 and adds 0.1 DASH to the registration fee as the perpetual and pre-programmed kinds do, and a second claim is error 40722). With a variable-rate distribution function, one claim covers at most 128 intervals.
- **Actions.** Mint, transfer (with a public, shared-encrypted or private note), burn, freeze, unfreeze, destroy frozen funds, claim, emergency action (pause and resume), config update, set a direct purchase price, direct purchase. Fees are paid in credits.
- **Non-transferable tokens** (protocol 14, config format 1; platform book, Non-Transferable Tokens, `data-model/non-transferable-tokens.html`, read 13 Nov 2026). Once an identity holds one, nobody can move it to another identity. It can still be minted, claimed, bought from the issuer, burned, frozen and paused as its rules allow, and it pays for documents of its own contract by being burned (`tokenCost` with `effect: 1`). Refused: a transfer (error 40726) · a document type that would pay it to the contract owner (10280) · any other contract charging it (40726; give this code for that case) · a shielded pool (10279) · the resale market (10281). 10261 is a different refusal that holds for every token: a document type may burn only a token of its own contract. The flag is fixed at creation. The book's uses: a usage allowance, points, a reputation score.
- **Pausing** (the emergency action) stops three things: transfers, every operation on the token's shielded pool, and from protocol 14 every document payment in that token, whether the payment would transfer the token or burn it (error 40711). An app whose documents are paid in a token stops taking them while that token is paused. The rest goes on under its own rules while a token is paused: minting to an identity, a claim, a burn sent as its own transition, a freeze, a config update, and a direct purchase from the issuer (the network's validation code and the protocol 14 change list, development branch, read 14 Nov 2026; the purchase check carries a comment saying this is by design, so an issuer can stop movement and still sell). So a pause stops movement and spending, and the supply can still change under it. One rule, `emergencyActionRules`, names who may pause and resume, and its default is no one: a token created with the default has nobody who may pause it. When a design depends on what a pause blocks, say where this comes from and have them try it on testnet.
- **Direct purchase is not a market.** The issuer (the contract owner or another authorized party) sets a price, and a buyer pays credits for tokens at that price. Each purchase mints new tokens to the buyer and the credits go to the contract owner, so direct sales add to the supply. A resale market between holders (`ask` and `bid` orders, the token market system contract) arrives with protocol version 15. Before 15 the network refuses `Tradeable` (error 10603), and it refuses it on a non-transferable token (10281). 10603 means the feature is not active yet, so it belongs to the time before protocol 15 and to nothing after it. A change made by someone the token's rules do not authorize is refused with `UnauthorizedTokenActionError`, code 40701. That is the code for a mint, a freeze, a pause or a configuration change by an identity that is not authorized, and for a change of the trade mode when no one may change it (platform source, `codes.rs` and `token_config_update_transition_action`, read 14 Nov 2026). So before protocol 15 the answer is 10603, and after it, with the default rules, it is 40701. **Decide it at creation.** `marketplaceRules` holds `tradeMode` and `tradeModeChangeRules`, which name who may change the mode later. The default is no one, and then the token keeps the mode it was created with: a token that was not created tradeable can join the resale market only if its issuer kept the right to change the mode. An issuer who wants that names who may change it when the token is created.
- **Token calls** (the book's basic token tutorial, `@dashevo/evo-sdk`). `sdk.tokens.mint({ dataContractId, tokenPosition, amount, recipientId, identityId, identityKey, signer })` · `transfer` (the same, with `senderId` in place of `identityId`) · `burn` · `claim({ ..., distributionType })` · reads: `identityBalances(identityId, [contractIds])`, `balances([identityIds], contractId)`, `calculateId(contractId, position)`, `totalSupply(tokenId)`. An amount is a `bigint` in base units (with 2 decimals, `10_000_00n` is 10,000.00), and a token transition is signed with a CRITICAL key. The config is built from SDK classes (`TokenConfiguration`, `TokenConfigurationConvention`, `ChangeControlRules`, `AuthorizedActionTakers`, `TokenDistributionRules`, `TokenMarketplaceRules`) and published as `tokens: { 0: tokenConfig }` in the `DataContract`.
- **Tokens as an app's money.** A document type can charge a token for an action (`tokenCost`): burned, or paid to the owner. With `actionFees` an app can charge credits too (§17).
- **Which contract holds the token.** The book suggests a contract of its own for a token, so its control groups and supply rules stay apart from an app's document types. One thing decides against that: a document type can burn only a token of its own contract (error 10261 for another contract's), and a non-transferable token can be charged only by its own contract. A token that documents burn lives in the same contract as those document types. Contracts that belong together can be tied in a contract group (§17).
- **NFTs are documents.** `transferable: 1` and `tradeMode: 1` on a document type let each document be transferred, priced and bought (`sdk.documents.transfer`, `setPrice`, `purchase`). `creationRestrictionMode`: 0 anyone, 1 the contract owner only, 2 no one. `documentsMutable: false` freezes the content. `canBeDeleted` decides whether one can be burned. Mutability is fixed when the type is defined. The docs' example is a `card` type with `attack` and `defense`: `documentsMutable: false`, `canBeDeleted: true`, `transferable: 1`, `tradeMode: 1`, `creationRestrictionMode: 1`.
- **Giving and selling a document** (platform book, Creation, Transfers and Trading, `contract-keywords/ownership-and-trading.html`). A transfer needs `transferable: 1` on the type (error 10404 without it). The owner signs it with the next `$revision`, the recipient does not have to agree, and a listed price is removed. A sale needs `tradeMode: 1`: the owner sets a price in credits, and any other identity buys by sending that exact price (a different price is error 40109, no price set is 40108, and an owner cannot buy its own document). The credits go from the buyer to the seller, the buyer becomes `$ownerId`, and the listing is removed. The two settings are independent, and both are fixed once the type exists (40212). Each of transfer, price update and purchase can carry its own token cost and action fee (§17). A type whose documents change hands uses `creatorRefersTo`, because `ownerRefersTo` is refused on it. A type with `creationRestrictionMode` 1 or 2 cannot give moderators the delete ability. **The calls** (Evo SDK reference for `@dashevo/evo-sdk` 5.0.3, https://dashpay.github.io/evo-sdk-website/docs.html, read 14 Nov 2026). All three start the same way: `const document = await sdk.documents.get(contractId, documentType, documentId)`, then `document.revision = BigInt(document.revision) + 1n`. To list it: `sdk.documents.setPrice({ document, price, identityKey, signer })`, signed by the owner; a price of `0n` takes it off sale. To buy it: `sdk.documents.purchase({ document, buyerId, price, identityKey, signer })`, signed by the buyer, with `price` equal to the listed price. To give it: `sdk.documents.transfer({ document, recipientId, identityKey, signer })`. `buyerId` and `recipientId` are `Identifier` objects (`Identifier.fromBase58(id)`). Each call also takes `tokenPaymentInfo`, `actionFeeAgreement` and `settings` when the type charges for the action. `price` is a `bigint` count of credits, and credits are whole numbers with no decimal part: 0.005 DASH is `500_000_000n`. Write a call in the shape this pack or a page you retrieved gives it. When neither has the shape, name the method and link the reference.
- **Which one.** Many equal units → a token. One of a kind with its own fields → a document type with trade mode. A fee, a membership or a ticket → often both: the token pays, the document proves.
