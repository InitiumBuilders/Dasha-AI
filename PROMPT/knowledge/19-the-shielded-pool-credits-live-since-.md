## 19. THE SHIELDED POOL (credits live since Platform 4.0, token pools since 5.0)

Zcash's Orchard design on Platform: Halo 2 proofs with no trusted setup, RedPallas signatures, Sinsemilla commitments. Announced 19 Feb 2026 (dash.org/blog); the roadmap lists shielded balances at v4.0, July 2026, and Dash announced them live on mainnet on 4 Aug 2026; per-token pools came with 5.0. It runs on Platform. Core keeps CoinJoin, which is a different tool.

- **Hidden inside the pool:** amounts, senders and recipients. **Still public:** nullifiers, note commitments, anchors, the proofs, and every transparent field: the amount entering or leaving the pool, the identity or address on the transparent side, the fee and the time.
- **Transitions:** 15 Shield · 16 Shielded Transfer · 17 Unshield · 18 Shield from Asset Lock (L1 DASH straight into the pool) · 19 Shielded Withdrawal (pool straight to L1) · 20 Identity Create from Shielded Pool · from protocol 14: 21 Shield from Identity · 22 Identity Top Up from Shielded Pool · 26 Token Shielded Transfer with Shielded Fee · 27 Token Unshield with Shielded Fee · 28 Token Purchase from Shielded Pool.
- **Limits:** at most 6 actions in a bundle · each shield input is at least 100,000 credits · a shielded withdrawal's `coreFeePerByte` is a non-zero Fibonacci number, 6,765 at most from protocol 14 · creating an identity from the pool uses fixed amounts (0.03, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 or 1 DASH at protocol 14, checked in the source on 13 Nov 2026; a wrong amount is error 10827), and the new identity starts with that amount less the metered cost of creating it and the proof fee · storage per action is 550 bytes at protocol 14 (344 before).
- **Token pools** (platform book, Token Shielded Pools, read 13 Nov 2026). Each token has its own pool, so its crowd is that token's holders alone, not everyone who uses a shielded pool. `hasShieldedPool` is fixed at creation. A pooled token must be transferable (error 10279), and its freeze, unfreeze and destroy rules must authorize no one (10278): tokens in a pool cannot be frozen or taken. Pausing still works: every pool operation is refused while the token is paused. Buying a token from the pool needs the credit pool to hold at least 250 notes.
- **The note floor.** `minimumPoolNotesForOutgoing` (250 at most) makes tokens wait until the pool holds that many notes before any can leave. It is absent by default, and absent means no floor: a new pool lets its first note leave at once, and a spend from a nearly empty pool can be linked to the shield that filled it. A floor has its own price: it holds the first depositors until enough notes arrive. It can be changed later only if `minimumPoolNotesForOutgoingChangeRules` names someone when the token is created. It counts notes, not holders, so one depositor can reach it alone and it guarantees no crowd. It applies to what leaves with a visible destination (an unshield, a burn from the pool, a document cost paid from the pool), not to transfers inside the pool.
- **Who is seen moving a pooled token.** Tokens cannot pay fees, so seven pool operations ride in a batch that an identity signs and pays for in credits: shield, unshield, shielded transfer, mint to pool (only where `mintingAllowChoosingDestination` is set), burn from pool, claim to pool, direct purchase to pool. The chain shows which identity acted on which token and when; what the notes hide is who holds what afterwards. Three transitions show no identity at all, because a second bundle pays the fee out of the credit pool: 26 (transfer), 27 (unshield) and 28 (purchase). Pool operations write no token history, and they still leave public traces: spent nullifiers, the pool's note count and its anchors. The six shielded pool queries take an optional token id and then answer for that token's pool.
- **A group burn from a pool has a deadline nothing announces.** The proposer's bundle names an anchor, and a pool in use drops an anchor after 1,000 blocks (protocol 14, checked in the source on 13 Nov 2026). The bundle cannot be rebuilt on a newer anchor without becoming a new proposal, and a group action has no expiry and no cancel. A proposal that has not gathered its signatures by then cannot close and cannot be withdrawn. So the group agrees first and signs well inside the window.
- **What the pool costs** (platform book, Shielded Transaction Fees; the constants are protocol 14, checked in the source on 13 Nov 2026). The minimum shielded fee is 40,000,000 credits for the bundle's proof plus 37,070,000 for each action (22,000,000 of processing and 550 bytes of storage at 27,400 a byte). A bundle has at least 2 actions. So: 2 actions 114,140,000 credits (0.0011414 DASH) · 3 actions 151,210,000 · 4 actions 188,280,000. A shielded transfer pays exactly that out of the pool, and paying more is refused. An unshield pays that plus 6,082,800 (120,222,800 at 2 actions), taken from the amount that leaves, so the address receives the amount less the fee. A shielded withdrawal to L1 pays that plus 112,340,000 (226,480,000 at 2 actions), taken the same way. A top up of an identity from the pool pays that plus 219,200. Shielding from an address or from an identity is metered like any write, plus 40,000,000 and 22,000,000 an action for the proof; an identity has to hold the amount plus an admission estimate (149,760,000 at 2 actions) or it is refused before the proof is checked. Shielding from an asset lock pays the minimum fee plus 50,000,000; what is left of the lock goes to `surplus_output`, or to fees when none is set, and then a surplus above 0.2 DASH is refused. A token pool operation in a batch is paid by the signing identity: the metered writes plus the same proof fee, and a proof that fails there is still charged. The three with no identity pay from the credit pool the minimum fee of both bundles (228,280,000 when each has 2 actions) plus small flat writes, and a failed proof there costs nothing. These constants are versioned: name protocol 14 with them, and check before quoting them for a later version.
- **The warnings that go with every answer:** a small pool hides little, because the privacy is the crowd · a high note threshold can strand early depositors until others arrive · amounts going in and out are visible, so the same amount in and out minutes apart links itself · timing, IP address and the frontend still leak · pool operations leave no token history. Privacy here depends on how it is used.
- **Planned, not live (v5.1):** anonymous contact requests, anonymous documents, payments to shielded usernames.
