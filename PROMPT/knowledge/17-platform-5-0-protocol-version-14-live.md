## 17. PLATFORM 5.0, PROTOCOL VERSION 14 (live on mainnet since 8 Nov 2026)

Sources: the Platform 5.0 post by Samuel Westrich, CTO of Dash Core Group (https://www.dash.org/blog/dash-platform-5/, 20 Oct 2026, part 1 of 3) and the 5.0 docs, read 12 Nov 2026. Version facts age: confirm with `dash_platform` `status` and `search_dash_docs` before a builder commits money to one.

- **How it went live.** Testnet from 15 Oct. Evonodes signalled in epoch 86 (from 21 Oct); more than 67% of them locked it in at the start of epoch 87 (30 Oct); protocol 14 took effect at the first block of epoch 88, on 8 Nov 2026. An epoch is 9 days 3 hours. A protocol version activates by evonode signalling at an epoch boundary, never on a calendar date, which is why a planned date can slip.
- **Relational contracts.** A field can point at an identity, a contract, a token, a key or another document (`refersTo` on a property; `ownerRefersTo` and `creatorRefersTo` on the document type), and the network checks the link when the document is written. Up to 256 links per document. `propertyConstraints` holds up to 16 field rules per document type. A field can be frozen after creation (`immutable`). Typed lists hold up to 1,024 items. Commit-then-reveal and field encryption are declared in the contract. This is the 5.0 answer to "I need the network to enforce a relationship": the rule lives in the schema, and still no code runs on chain.
- **Apps that earn (`actionFees`).** Declared on the document type: an `actionFees` object with an optional `pricing` and any of `create`, `replace`, `delete`, `transfer`, `update_price` and `purchase`, each holding an `owner` amount and/or a `moderators` amount in credits, charged on top of the network fee. They are two separate amounts, not a percentage. The platform book's example: `"create": { "moderators": 100000000, "owner": 10000000 }` adds 0.001 DASH for the moderation team and 0.0001 DASH for the owner to each create. A `moderators` amount needs moderation declared in the contract (error 10902), and an action whose parts are all 0 is refused: leave it out. `pricing`: `"fixed"` charges the amounts as written; `"feeMultiplier"`, the default, scales them by the fee multiplier of the epoch the action runs in, which is 1 today. Each amount goes into its pot, and none of it goes to the network. A pot is paid out by a Contract Fee Claim (transition 25), once per epoch at most: the owner pot to the contract owner, the moderators pot split equally between the team (the owner alone when no moderator is appointed; an elected team splits by its charter), and any member may claim for all (JS SDK: `contracts.feePots`, `contracts.claimFees`). **Fixed for a document type once it exists:** a contract update cannot add, change or remove the fees of an existing document type or switch their pricing (error 40212). A document type added by an update may declare its own, so a live contract gains fees only through new document types. **The signer agrees to the fee.** Every transition on an action that charges one carries `$actionFeeAgreement` with the `owner` and `moderators` amounts the signer saw and, for `feeMultiplier` pricing, `feeMultiplier: { knownPermille, increaseTolerancePercent }` (1000 is 1x; 20 accepts up to 1.2x). No agreement is error 40132, other amounts 40133, a multiplier above the tolerance 40134; each bumps the nonce and charges no action fee. So a fee cannot rise under a user. Whoever pays the network fee pays the action fee, a contract owner does not pay the `owner` part, only an action that executes is charged, and the fee counts against the budget of a limited key when its identity pays it.

- **Token costs and free usage (`tokenCost`).** A document type can make an action cost tokens (since protocol 9): for any of the same six actions, a cost object with `tokenPosition`, `amount`, an optional `contractId` (another contract's token), `effect` (0 sends the tokens to the contract owner, 1 burns them; a non-transferable token can only be burned) and, acted on from protocol 14, `gasFeesPaidBy` (0 the document owner, 1 the contract owner, 2 prefer the contract owner) and `optional`. The transition carries `$tokenPaymentInfo` (`tokenContractPosition`, `maximumTokenCost`, and `gasFeesPaidBy`: `"DocumentOwner"`, `"ContractOwner"` or `"PreferContractOwner"`). **Sponsored gas:** when the cost offers it and the signer asks for it, the contract owner pays the storage and processing fee of that action, and any action fee with it. Only an action paid with a token can be sponsored. If the owner's balance falls short, a transition that insisted is refused unpaid (error 40222) and one that only preferred falls back to the signer; asking for what the cost does not offer is error 40129; a transition that fails validation is not sponsored, and its signer pays for the work that ran. With `optional: true` a transition may skip the token and pay the network fee in credits. Together these are the book's "free usage" pattern: an app hands out tokens, users act for free while their tokens last, and keep going on credits after. A type that offers to pay should bound its documents' size (`maxLength`, `maxItems`, `maxBytes`), because the owner pays the storage of every sponsored document. A cost cannot be added, changed or removed on an existing document type (error 40212).
- **Apps that moderate.** A contract opts in with `moderation` in its config, set at creation: which lists it keeps (`banlist`, `suspensions`, `warnings`) and who moderates (`moderators`): the contract owner, up to 16 appointed identities, or a team elected by masternodes and evonodes under a charter in the moderation-charters system contract. An update cannot add or remove a list (error 40002). Moderators act alone, with no threshold, and the owner may always moderate. What they can do (Contract User Moderation, transition 24): ban, unban, suspend, unsuspend, warn, clear warnings, delete a document, restore a document, change a document's fields. Deleting documents needs `moderatorAbilities` with `"delete": true` on the document type. A deleted document can be restored within a week (error 41120 after that). Every entry carries its reason, up to 1,024 bytes, and anyone can read it. A banned identity's document transitions on that contract are refused (41107), except deletions; a suspended one is refused until the suspension ends (41108); transfers to and purchases from a barred identity are refused (41114). Token transitions are not affected, and a warning blocks nothing. The moderators can be changed by a contract update; the lists cannot. Neither the owner nor a moderator can be banned or suspended, and a named moderator has to exist (error 41110). A suspension names the block time at which it lapses, in milliseconds, and an identity carries at most 16 warnings.
- **What moderators may do to documents (`moderatorAbilities`)** (platform book, Moderator Abilities, `contract-keywords/moderator-abilities.html`, read 13 Nov 2026). Set on a document type, in a contract whose config declares moderation, and fixed once the type exists (40212); a type added by an update may declare it. `delete: true` lets the moderators delete documents of the type. By default a deletion leaves a removal record and can be restored within a week; `deleteKeepsRecord: false` leaves none and is final. `deleteWithin` limits deletion to that many seconds after the document's last change. `deleteRefundsOwner` returns the deleted document's storage to whoever paid for it (default false). `deleteKeepsFields` names fields whose values stay public in the removal record. `changeFields` lists top-level properties that only the moderators write, such as a report's `status`. Each listed property has to be optional, stored, not `immutable`, not a reference or read by one, and in no contested index. A document's owner cannot set or change those fields (error 41124) unless the owner is a moderator. A moderator writes them with the moderation transition's `changeDocumentFields` action and a reason (41123 for a field that is not listed, 41101 for a signer who is not a moderator, 10905 for a change that changes nothing). The document then carries `$moderatedAt` and `$moderatedBy`, which an index may name, and its `$revision` goes up by one. The moderator pays for the transition. A listed property is left out of `required`: only a moderator can set it, so every document starts without it, and a type that requires one is refused. An index may name `$moderatedAt` and `$moderatedBy` on a type that lists `changeFields` (a unique index may not), and neither of the two is written in `required`. `deleteWithin` counts from `$updatedAt`, so a type whose documents can be replaced lists `$updatedAt` in `required`. A contract whose types give moderators an ability may keep no list at all. The book's example, whole:

```json
"report": {
  "type": "object",
  "documentsMutable": false,
  "moderatorAbilities": { "delete": true, "changeFields": ["status", "resolution"] },
  "indices": [
    { "name": "byStatus", "properties": [{ "status": "asc" }, { "$createdAt": "asc" }] }
  ],
  "properties": {
    "postId": { "type": "identifier", "position": 0, "refersTo": { "type": "deletableDocument", "documentType": "post" } },
    "reason": { "type": "integer", "minimum": 0, "maximum": 8, "position": 1 },
    "status": { "type": "integer", "minimum": 1, "maximum": 3, "position": 2 },
    "resolution": { "type": "string", "maxLength": 200, "position": 3 }
  },
  "required": ["$createdAt", "postId", "reason"],
  "additionalProperties": false
}
```

A user files a report and cannot edit it. A report starts with no `status`. A moderator sets `status` and `resolution` when it is dealt with, finds the open ones through `byStatus`, and everyone can see what became of it. **When you show a document type, show it whole,** as this one is: `"type": "object"`, a `position` on every property, `additionalProperties: false`. A cut-down copy will not register. To show one keyword alone, show that key and its value with no `properties` beside it, and say it is a part.
- **A value awarded by vote (a `contested` index)** (platform book, Contested Indexes, `contract-keywords/contested.html`, and Contested Documents, `data-model/contested-documents.html`, read 13 Nov 2026). Any contract can use what DPNS uses for short names: a unique index marked `contested` turns a race for a scarce value into a vote of masternodes and evonodes, where an evonode's vote counts four times. `fieldMatches` says which values are contested, with a regular expression on a property (write `^` and `$` to match the whole value; with no `fieldMatches`, every value is contested). A create for a contested value carries `$prefundedVotingBalance`: the index name and the most credits the contender will pay. The fund is 0.1 DASH, and from protocol 14 it doubles at 250 contenders and at every 50 after, up to a cap of 1,000 contenders (error 40141). Others can join for one week on mainnet, and the vote runs two weeks. `resolution` says what the voters may choose. 0, as DPNS has it: each voter picks a contender, abstains, or votes to lock the value so that nobody gets it, and a value that ended locked refuses new contests (40110). The lock is a vote, cast by masternodes and evonodes; a contender has no way to lock a value. 1, from protocol 14: the voters pick a contender or abstain and there is no lock to vote for, so a contest ends with a winner, and a lone contender is awarded the value when the join window closes. 2, from protocol 15: each document is its own contender, so one identity can enter several. A tie goes to the earliest contender. At registration: the index is unique, the type sets `documentsMutable: false` (10248), it is the type's only unique index (10249), and the type cannot set `ttl` or let moderators delete. Errors a builder meets: 40114 no fund or too little · 40111 the join window has passed · 40112 the identity already contends · 40118 the pair names another index · 40119 a fund sent for a value that is not contested.
- **Contract settings decided once** (platform book, Contract-Level Keys and config, `contract-keywords/contract-config.html`). Almost every `config` key is fixed at registration (error 40002 on a change): `readonly` (true freezes the contract at its first version, only a create can set it, and from protocol 15 the configuration of its tokens is frozen with it), `keepsHistory` (every version of the contract is kept and provable), the three document defaults, and which moderation lists are kept. `sizedIntegerTypes` can be turned on and not off. A contract needs at least one document type unless it defines tokens (10214). Each update raises `version` by exactly one (10212). `keywords` (50 at most, each 3 to 50 bytes with no spaces) and `description` (3 to 100 bytes) make a contract findable through the keyword search contract, and both can change. A contract that will ever need moderation declares it when it is created.
- **Expiring documents (`ttl`).** `ttl` is a number of seconds on the document type, from one hour to one year. It is the same for every document of the type, cannot be added, changed or removed later, and the type must list `$createdAt` in `required`. A document expires at `$createdAt` plus `ttl`; after each block the network deletes expired documents, oldest first, up to 128 a block. Nothing extends a life: a replace or a transfer does not move the expiry, and an expired document cannot be replaced, transferred, bought or restored (error 40140). **The cost in one line, for any summary:** an expiring document pays a small price per byte for its lifetime and prepays its own deletion, at least 1,200,000 credits, so a 100 byte document that lives an hour costs about half of a permanent one (1,242,100 credits against 2,700,000) and a 1,000 byte one about a seventeenth (1,621,000 against 27,000,000). The price per byte and the prepayment go in the same sentence, every time. Storage per byte, by lifetime: 1 credit up to an hour, 4 up to a day, 8 up to two days, 15 up to four days, 26 up to seven days, and beyond that 34 for each 9.125-day period the lifetime spans, rounded up (a year is 1,360); permanent storage is 27,000. An expiring document also prepays its own deletion: 1,200,000 credits, plus 400,000 for each index level (an index counts one level for each of its properties), plus 420 per byte of the document. For a small document that prepayment is most of the cost, so give both parts. Compare the two with arithmetic: a permanent document costs 27,000 × its bytes; one that expires within the hour costs 421 × its bytes + 1,200,000 (+ 400,000 for each index level). They meet at about 45 bytes with no index, and each index level moves that point up by about 15 bytes. Above it the expiring document is the cheaper one, and the gap widens with every byte; a longer lifetime narrows it (a year is 1,780 × bytes + the same prepayment). A stored document is rarely that small, since its two identifiers alone are 64 bytes, so at most real sizes the expiring document costs less. Work the sum for the builder's own size before saying which is cheaper. What a builder gives up with `ttl` is the data: at expiry it is gone, and a type with `ttl` keeps no history. Expiry refunds no storage. `ttl` is refused together with `documentsKeepHistory: true`, `indexOnly: true` or a contested index. `canBeDeleted: false` stops only the owner; expiry still removes the document. A later replace, transfer or purchase pays for the bytes it adds at the price of the lifetime left. Until the cleanup reaches an expired document it still holds its values in unique indexes, so a create with the same unique value is refused as a duplicate. A `timeRange` index can carry a `ttl` of its own, which expires index entries and leaves the documents in place: that is a different feature.
- **Queries, all with proofs.** Ranked indexes (top or bottom K by count, sum or average), time-window indexes, counter-only document types (`indexOnly`: kept as index entries only), `having` on an aggregate, chained and composite queries. 100 results per query at most. The exact syntax is in §2.
- **Keys with limits.** An authentication key below the master level can carry `totalBudget` (credits) and `expiresAt` (block time, in milliseconds). Transfer, encryption and decryption keys cannot. **The budget rule** (platform book, Key Budgets and Expiry, `data-model/key-limits.html`, read 12 Nov 2026): what is known before a transition runs has to fit in what is left of the budget, or the transition is refused unpaid (error 40218). That is four amounts: credits the transition moves out of the identity (a purchase price, a prefunded voting balance, and an action fee when the signer's identity pays it), the storage fee, a fee priced up front (the book's example is a contract registration), and the signer's own fee increase (`userFeeIncrease`: extra processing the signer chose to add, a percentage up to 65,535; it is not the network fee itself). Only the metered processing fee, known after the run, can take a key past its budget; what is left then stops at zero and the key is spent. The book's worked case is a transition with 50,000,000 of storage and 2,000,000 of metered processing, and nothing else. Its known cost, the part that has to fit, is the 50,000,000 of storage alone: the processing fee is not part of that test, because it is not known yet. With 60,000,000 left it runs, the identity pays 52,000,000 and 8,000,000 is left. With 50,000,001 left it runs, the identity pays 52,000,000 and the key ends at zero. With 49,999,999 left it is refused with 40218 and nothing is charged. With nothing left it is refused with 20015. So what is left after a transition is what was left before, less everything the identity paid for it, stopping at zero. A key that still holds something and cannot cover the next transition's known cost gets 40218; 20015 is only for a key at zero. The 2,000,000 of processing is the book's example: in a person's own case the processing fee is whatever the run meters. So a write runs whenever its known cost fits in what is left, and what is left afterwards is that amount less everything paid, the processing fee included. For a person's own numbers, send the subtraction to `calculate`. A failed transition that is still paid for spends from the budget like a success. A storage refund does not restore budget. A spent key is refused (error 20015), and so is an expired one (20016); a key signs until one millisecond before `expiresAt`. These refusals are unpaid: the balance, the budget and the nonce stay as they were. Registering: limits on any other kind of key are refused (10536), a zero budget is refused (10537), and an expiry already in the past is a paid failure (40219), most often seconds given where milliseconds are expected. A key with limits cannot be registered while an identity is created from the shielded pool (10538); add it by identity update afterwards. A limited key cannot sign a limits update (20017), and from protocol 15 it cannot sign a contract create or update (20018). What is left of a budget is read with `sdk.identities.keysRemainingBudgets(identityId, keyIds)`; treat the number as an upper bound, because a transition already waiting can spend first. The book also says some SDK bindings did not expose limits when it was written, so check the installed SDK before promising a call. An Identity Key Limits Update (transition 23) only loosens: it raises a budget or moves an expiry later. It cannot lower a limit or give one to a key that has none; to tighten, disable the key and register another. Its signer is a master key, or a critical key with no limits and no bounds. `contractBounds` is a separate restriction (`singleContract`, `documentType`, `contractGroup`), and a bound authentication key signs batch transitions only (error 20013). The 5.0 post describes the App Connect system contract as wallet login built on this: an app gets a key that can touch only its own contract and spend only what the user allowed.
- **Contract groups** (platform book, Contract Groups, `data-model/contract-groups.html`, read 13 Nov 2026). A set of contracts, document types and tokens that one identity owns, recorded in consensus state, so an app made of several contracts can prove they belong together. It is not a token's control group (§18), which is a set of identities with voting power inside one contract. The rules: a group is registered inside a data contract create transition (format version 1), and its id comes from the owner and the identity nonce, so it is known before broadcast and one transition can register a group and join it · the owner can name up to 16 admins, existing identities who each act alone, with no threshold · a member is a whole contract, one document type or one token, and only the create transition of the joining contract can declare it, signed by the group's owner or an admin · a contract declares at most 16 memberships · memberships are append-only: nothing leaves, and a contract that already exists cannot join later · a name is 1 to 64 characters, a description 1 to 256. Errors: 10360 to 10367 at structure, unpaid (10365 is unused) · 41000 the group exists · 41001 not found · 41002 the signer is neither owner nor admin · 41003 an admin was not found; the 41000s are paid. The fee is ordinary storage at the standard rate. Reads: `getContractGroupInfo`, `getContractGroupMembers` (in pages) and `getContractGroupsForContract`, which `sdk.contractGroups` wraps in the JavaScript SDK. Registering or joining a group has no SDK option yet: the book says it still takes a hand-built create transition. A key bound to a group (`contractBounds: contractGroup`) signs batches only, on any member, and its reach grows as the group grows. A token's control group cannot own a contract group yet.
- **Private tokens.** A token can have its own shielded pool (§19). The pool flag is fixed at creation; the pool's note floor can change later only if its change rule names someone at creation.
- **Names and votes.** The fund to contest a premium name fell from 0.2 to 0.1 DASH, and a masternode vote on a contest from 0.0001 to 0.00002 DASH. A tie goes to the earliest contender. A contest holds at most 1,000 contenders; the fund doubles at 250 contenders and at every 50 after.
- **Withdrawals.** The network-wide daily limit is 15% of the previous day's credits with a floor of 500 DASH (it was a flat 2,000 DASH). A second limit follows Core's credit pool (floor 1,500 DASH).
- **Under it.** GroveDB 6.1, support for Dash Core 24, `@dashevo/evo-sdk` 5.0 and the 5.0 Rust SDK.

### What breaks at protocol 14 (the upgrade checklist)
Say who each part is for. Items 1 to 4 are for anyone with an app or a contract. Item 5 is for people who run a node, and it is the only one for them. Item 6 is for everyone.
1. **Update the SDK first.** Document IDs and document storage changed, and so did the proof format. A document built by a pre-5.0 SDK is refused, and an old client cannot check new proofs. `npm install @dashevo/evo-sdk@latest`, Node 22 or newer.
2. **No hyphen in names.** A property name or document type name can no longer contain `-`.
3. **Integers are fixed.** An update cannot change an integer's size or sign. Unsigned integer indexes now sort by value.
4. **Transient values are never stored.** Nothing should rely on reading one back.
5. **Node operators.** `npm install -g dashmate`, then `dashmate update`, then a full stop and start. A node left on 4.x stopped at activation.
6. **Not live yet.** The token resale market (protocol 15), anonymous documents and contact requests (v5.1), smart contracts (v6.0, tentative).
