## 17. PLATFORM 5.0, PROTOCOL VERSION 14 (live on mainnet since 8 Nov 2026)

Sources: the Platform 5.0 post by Samuel Westrich, CTO of Dash Core Group (https://www.dash.org/blog/dash-platform-5/, 20 Oct 2026, part 1 of 3) and the 5.0 docs, read 12 Nov 2026. Version facts age: confirm with `dash_platform` `status` and `search_dash_docs` before a builder commits money to one.

- **How it went live.** Testnet from 15 Oct. Evonodes signalled in epoch 86 (from 21 Oct); more than 67% of them locked it in at the start of epoch 87 (30 Oct); protocol 14 took effect at the first block of epoch 88, on 8 Nov 2026. An epoch is 9 days 3 hours. A protocol version activates by evonode signalling at an epoch boundary, never on a calendar date, which is why a planned date can slip.
- **Relational contracts.** A field can point at an identity, a contract, a token, a key or another document (`refersTo` on a property; `ownerRefersTo` and `creatorRefersTo` on the document type), and the network checks the link when the document is written. Up to 256 links per document. `propertyConstraints` holds up to 16 field rules per document type. A field can be frozen after creation (`immutable`). Typed lists hold up to 1,024 items. Commit-then-reveal and field encryption are declared in the contract. This is the 5.0 answer to "I need the network to enforce a relationship": the rule lives in the schema, and still no code runs on chain.
- **Apps that earn (`actionFees`).** Declared on the document type: an `actionFees` object with any of `create`, `replace`, `delete`, `transfer`, `update_price` and `purchase`, each holding an `owner` amount and/or a `moderators` amount in credits. They are two separate amounts, never a percentage, and a `moderators` amount needs moderation switched on in the contract. `pricing` is `"feeMultiplier"` (the default) or `"fixed"`: read the keyword page before choosing. Each amount goes into its pot. A pot is paid out by a Contract Fee Claim (transition 25), once per epoch at most: the owner pot to the contract owner, the moderators pot shared equally by the team. A contract update cannot add, change or remove a fee (error 40212), and the signer states the fee they accept (`$actionFeeAgreement`), so a fee cannot rise under a user. When an owner sponsors the network fee of a token-paid action, the owner pays only the `moderators` part.
- **Apps that moderate.** A contract opts in with `moderation` in its config, set at creation: which lists it keeps (`banlist`, `suspensions`, `warnings`) and who moderates (`moderators`): the contract owner, up to 16 appointed identities, or a team elected by masternodes and evonodes under a charter in the moderation-charters system contract. An update cannot add or remove a list (error 40002). Moderators act alone, with no threshold, and the owner may always moderate. What they can do (Contract User Moderation, transition 24): ban, unban, suspend, unsuspend, warn, clear warnings, delete a document, restore a document, change a document's fields. Deleting documents needs `moderatorAbilities` with `"delete": true` on the document type. A deleted document can be restored within a week (error 41120 after that). Every entry carries its reason, up to 1,024 bytes, and anyone can read it. A banned identity's document transitions on that contract are refused (41107), except deletions; a suspended one is refused until the suspension ends (41108); transfers to and purchases from a barred identity are refused (41114). Token transitions are not affected, and a warning blocks nothing.
- **Expiring documents (`ttl`).** `ttl` is a number of seconds on the document type, from one hour to one year. It is the same for every document of the type, cannot be added, changed or removed later, and the type must list `$createdAt` in `required`. A document expires at `$createdAt` plus `ttl`; after each block the network deletes expired documents, oldest first, up to 128 a block. Nothing extends a life: a replace or a transfer does not move the expiry, and an expired document cannot be replaced, transferred, bought or restored (error 40140). **The cost in one line, for any summary:** an expiring document pays a small price per byte for its lifetime and prepays its own deletion, at least 1,200,000 credits, so a 100 byte document that lives an hour costs about half of a permanent one (1,242,100 credits against 2,700,000) and a 1,000 byte one about a seventeenth (1,621,000 against 27,000,000). The price per byte and the prepayment go in the same sentence, every time. Storage per byte, by lifetime: 1 credit up to an hour, 4 up to a day, 8 up to two days, 15 up to four days, 26 up to seven days, and beyond that 34 for each 9.125-day period the lifetime spans, rounded up (a year is 1,360); permanent storage is 27,000. An expiring document also prepays its own deletion: 1,200,000 credits, plus 400,000 for each index level, plus 420 per byte. For a small document that prepayment is most of the cost, so give both parts. Compare the two with arithmetic: a permanent document costs 27,000 × its bytes; one that expires within the hour costs 421 × its bytes + 1,200,000 (+ 400,000 for each index level). They meet at about 45 bytes with no index, and each index level moves that point up by about 15 bytes. Above it the expiring document is the cheaper one, and the gap widens with every byte; a longer lifetime narrows it (a year is 1,780 × bytes + the same prepayment). A stored document is rarely that small, since its two identifiers alone are 64 bytes, so at most real sizes the expiring document costs less. Work the sum for the builder's own size before saying which is cheaper. What a builder gives up with `ttl` is the data: at expiry it is gone, and a type with `ttl` keeps no history. Expiry refunds no storage. `ttl` is refused together with `documentsKeepHistory: true`, `indexOnly: true` or a contested index. `canBeDeleted: false` stops only the owner; expiry still removes the document.
- **Queries, all with proofs.** Ranked indexes (top or bottom K by count, sum or average), time-window indexes, counter-only document types (`indexOnly`: kept as index entries only), `having` on an aggregate, chained and composite queries. 100 results per query at most. The exact syntax is in §2.
- **Keys with limits.** An authentication key below the master level can carry `totalBudget` (credits) and `expiresAt` (block time, in milliseconds). Transfer, encryption and decryption keys cannot. **The budget rule** (platform book, Key Budgets and Expiry, read 12 Nov 2026): what is known before a transition runs has to fit in what is left of the budget, or the transition is refused unpaid (error 40218). That is four amounts: credits the transition moves out of the identity (a purchase price, a prefunded voting balance), the storage fee, a fee priced up front (the book's example is a contract registration), and the signer's own fee increase. Give those examples and no others. Only the metered processing fee, known after the run, can take a key past its budget; what is left then stops at zero and the key is spent. The book's worked case is a transition with 50,000,000 of storage and 2,000,000 of metered processing, and nothing else. With 60,000,000 left it runs, the identity pays 52,000,000 and 8,000,000 is left. With 50,000,001 left it runs, the identity pays 52,000,000 and the key ends at zero. With 49,999,999 left it is refused with 40218 and nothing is charged. With nothing left it is refused with 20015. So what is left after a transition is what was left before, less everything the identity paid for it, stopping at zero. A key that still holds something and cannot cover the next transition's known cost gets 40218; 20015 is only for a key at zero. For a person's own numbers, send the subtraction to `calculate`. A failed transition that is still paid for spends from the budget like a success. A storage refund does not restore budget. A spent key is refused (error 20015), and so is an expired one (20016); a key signs until one millisecond before `expiresAt`. These refusals are unpaid: the balance, the budget and the nonce stay as they were. Registering: limits on any other kind of key are refused (10536), a zero budget is refused (10537), and an expiry already in the past is a paid failure (40219), most often seconds given where milliseconds are expected. A key with limits cannot be registered while an identity is created from the shielded pool (10538); add it by identity update afterwards. A limited key cannot sign a limits update (20017), and from protocol 15 it cannot sign a contract create or update (20018). What is left of a budget is read with `sdk.identities.keysRemainingBudgets(identityId, keyIds)`; treat the number as an upper bound, because a transition already waiting can spend first. The book also says some SDK bindings did not expose limits when it was written, so check the installed SDK before promising a call. An Identity Key Limits Update (transition 23) only loosens: it raises a budget or moves an expiry later. It cannot lower a limit or give one to a key that has none; to tighten, disable the key and register another. Its signer is a master key, or a critical key with no limits and no bounds. `contractBounds` is a separate restriction (`singleContract`, `documentType`, `contractGroup`), and a bound authentication key signs batch transitions only (error 20013). The 5.0 post describes the App Connect system contract as wallet login built on this: an app gets a key that can touch only its own contract and spend only what the user allowed.
- **Private tokens.** A token can have its own shielded pool (§19). The setting is fixed at creation.
- **Names and votes.** The fund to contest a premium name fell from 0.2 to 0.1 DASH, and a masternode vote on a contest from 0.0001 to 0.00002 DASH. A tie goes to the earliest contender. A contest holds at most 1,000 contenders; the fund doubles at 250 contenders and at every 50 after.
- **Withdrawals.** The network-wide daily limit is 15% of the previous day's credits with a floor of 500 DASH (it was a flat 2,000 DASH). A second limit follows Core's credit pool (floor 1,500 DASH).
- **Under it.** GroveDB 6.1, support for Dash Core 24, `@dashevo/evo-sdk` 5.0 and the 5.0 Rust SDK.

### What breaks at protocol 14 (the upgrade checklist)
1. **Update the SDK first.** Document IDs and document storage changed, and so did the proof format. A document built by a pre-5.0 SDK is refused, and an old client cannot check new proofs. `npm install @dashevo/evo-sdk@latest`, Node 22 or newer.
2. **No hyphen in names.** A property name or document type name can no longer contain `-`.
3. **Integers are fixed.** An update cannot change an integer's size or sign. Unsigned integer indexes now sort by value.
4. **Transient values are never stored.** Nothing should rely on reading one back.
5. **Operators.** `npm install -g dashmate`, then `dashmate update`, then a full stop and start. A node left on 4.x stopped at activation.
6. **Not live yet.** The token resale market (protocol 15), anonymous documents and contact requests (v5.1), smart contracts (v6.0, tentative).
